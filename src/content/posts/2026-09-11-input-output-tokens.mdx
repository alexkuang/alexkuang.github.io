In the last few months I've seen a number of pieces on AI content and the sort of... lack of substance it has.
[One](https://www.experimental-history.com/p/i-like-em-thick) describes the idea of "thickness" -- writing feels "thick"
when you can feel all of the background, all the research, all the roads not taken, even in the end result.  In
contrast, AI writing is "thin" because it has... none of that.

I think of "thickness" (or its inverse, sloppiness) as the ratio between input and output tokens.  At the bottom of
the barrel, you have "write me a post about growth metrics" => 5 paragraphs of text.  Complete and utter slop.  But
as you increase the input tokens, the output gets thicker, and less slop-like.  e.g. "help me write a tweet around this
idea for growth metrics: (5 paragraphs of narration)" => 200 characters.  Or "here's a draft of a post I wrote along
with all of its resources, anything that could help reinforce my point that I should include?" => suggestions + edits to
the draft.

This doesn't mean you have to spend all day typing to get thick output.  The positive leverage from AI comes in from
*reusing your input tokens*.  e.g. if you write down your principles of good software design once, that can be piped
into all future prompts, automatically increasing your input to output ratio.  This is the entire mechanic of AGENTS.md
and skills.

What's the cutoff of the input-output ratio for "not slop" / "actually valuable"?  Input definitely can't be
be < output.  It's definitely not 1:1 either, because there are [no lossless
transformations](https://sophiebits.com/2026/06/25/there-are-no-lossless-transformations-of-natural-language-text) when
it comes to AI..  But is it... 2:1?  3:1?  10:1?  How does one count when the "type" of the tokens changes, e.g.
natural language => code, or text => audio?  Maybe it's all just vibes in the end.
